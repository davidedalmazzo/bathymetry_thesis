# Block15J — protocol frozen before simulation

Scope is fully synthetic complex coefficients on the fixed BP12-style Fourier support; no real Vandenberg coefficient is read. The primary statistic is `log(E_[12,23)/E_[0.6,2.0))`, where each `E` is the equal-pair mean of support-weighted `|z_b-z_a|^2`, divided by the median temporal support energy. Lag classes are `[0.6,2)`, `[2,5)`, `[5,12)`, `[12,23)` s (approximately `[0.027,0.091)`, `[0.091,0.227)`, `[0.227,0.545)`, `[0.545,1.0)` of the fixed observation span). A class with fewer than three pairs, or a near-zero energy denominator, is invalid and causes abstention.

Support weights are uniform and fixed on a 17x7 window-response patch. Pairs, classes and bins share looks/windowed pixels and are not independent observations; the complete Monte-Carlo sequence is the counting unit. The Tukey response is applied once. No phase unwrap, truth-frequency alignment, transfer division, or real-data quantity is available to the diagnostic.

The rule detects only B: select B if the score is at or below the calibration A 5th empirical percentile, otherwise abstain. This conservatively targets at most 10% A-to-B calibration false alarms. It never calls A. Calibration is 24 independent sequences (A, observed-static B, OU-slow B; eight each; seed 151000); evaluation is 48 sequences (each family, radial/tangential held-out separation, eight each; seed 151001). Noise amplitude is 0.1 of the primary and alternates iid and stationary complex OU tau=5 s. A uses three finite-depth-dispersive modes, while B has one propagating primary plus an observed constant or slow nearby coefficient. Ratios/calibration separation are excluded from evaluation.

The utility gate is fixed: evaluation A-to-B <=10%, B detection >=50%, decision fraction >=40%, and B detection >=40% on held-out geometry. Failure stops the block. The frozen Block15I persistent-score rule is evaluated on exactly these sequences without recalibration. A constant *observed* offset cancels from increments; a source-static term does not if its observation transfer varies in time. Thus the primary experiment has constant transfer and cannot claim transfer-robust real-data discrimination. Analytic identities and variable-transfer non-cancellation are tests, not a favorable classifier.
